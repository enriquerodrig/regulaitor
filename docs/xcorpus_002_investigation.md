## For TFM memoria — diagnosed-but-deferred ceiling (WHAT/WHY/HOW/IMPACT)

This section is the memoria-ready synthesis (per the `feedback_optimization_narrative_doc` discipline). Negative findings ARE memoria-worthy.

- **WHAT** — diagnosed: standard `BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3` cross-encoder fails on cross-corpus multi-regulation queries (single-article dominance pattern).
- **WHY** — measured evidence (Call 1+2+3 above): for the personal-data-breach query spanning NIS2 + RGPD, the reranker scores 5 different paragraphs of NIS2 art 23 (the "incident notification" article) higher than any chunk of NIS2 art 35 (cross-reference to data-protection authorities) or GDPR art 33 (72-hour breach notification). Lowering `purity_threshold` 0.6→0.5 produces an IDENTICAL emitted set — the gate cannot help because top-5 is already pure NIS2 art 23 before the gate fires. The dense retriever DID find all 3 expected articles in the top-200 candidate pool, so dense retrieval is not the bottleneck.
- **HOW** — not implemented in v0.1.9. Three candidate fixes identified for follow-up microhitos: (A) per-article deduplication cap in purity gate (max N chunks per article in top-`top_k`; trivial ceremony, $0); (B) MMR / Maximal Marginal Relevance penalty in the reranker stage (`score = bge_score − λ × max_cosine_sim_to_already_ranked`; medium ceremony, $0); (C) hybrid `bge_score + article_diversity_bonus` (medium-large ceremony, $0). Option A is the most surgical and is the recommended next intervention.
- **IMPACT** — diagnosed-but-deferred: v0.1.9 does NOT change production defaults. The 1/3 expected articles surfaced (NIS2 23 only) is unchanged. The single paid validation deferred to `v0.1.16` will measure the impact of whichever fix (A/B/C) gets implemented before that point. v0.1.9's contribution is the diagnostic + the regression-pin slow test + the deferred-ceiling documentation that closes H15.1's open question on what specifically was failing.
